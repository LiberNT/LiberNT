# LiberNT trademark and brand policy

The LiberNT source code is free software under the [GNU GPL 3.0](COPYING). The **LiberNT** name and the LiberNT logo are not part of that licence. A free software licence grants rights in the code; it grants no rights in the name or the marks of the project that publishes it.

This policy says what anyone may do with the name and the logo without asking, and what needs permission. It exists so that a build called LiberNT is one the project stands behind, and so that nobody can pass work off as ours.

## What the policy covers

- The word mark **LiberNT**, alone or in a compound such as "LiberNT Setup".
- The LiberNT logo and the banner, in every size, colour and file format, including the versions shipped inside the operating system and in the boot screens.
- Any mark, name or design so close to those that it would be mistaken for them.

These marks belong to the LiberNT project and its founder. The logo and the banner are works protected by copyright, held by their author, all rights reserved. Although they live in this repository next to the source code, **they are not licensed under the GPL** and may not be copied, modified or redistributed outside the permissions below.

## What you may do without asking

- **Say what your software, article, video or course is about.** You may use the name to refer truthfully to this project: "runs on LiberNT", "a driver for LiberNT", "building LiberNT from source", "a review of LiberNT". That is ordinary referential use and it needs no permission.
- **Redistribute an official build unchanged.** You may mirror or hand on a release exactly as published, under its name and with its logo, including the ISO and disk images from the releases page.
- **Build the sources for yourself.** A build you make and keep, or hand to a colleague for testing, may carry the name.
- **Link to us** with the logo as the link's image, at its published proportions.
- **Quote the logo in editorial context**, for example in an article, a talk, a package listing or a comparison table, at a size and position that does not suggest we wrote the surrounding material.

## What needs our written permission

- **Shipping modified code under the name.** If you change the sources and distribute the result, rename it. Keep the attribution the GPL requires, in prose — "based on the LiberNT sources" is fine — but do not call the product LiberNT and do not ship the logo in it. This is the same rule the large free software projects use, and it is the one that matters most: a user who boots something called LiberNT must be getting our code, not a variant we have never seen.
- **Any name, logo or icon built from ours**: a redraw, a recolour, an overlay, a mascot, a derived wordmark, a font-matched lookalike.
- **Domain names, repository names, package names, app store listings, social accounts and channel names** that lead with the mark, for instance `libernt-<something>.com` or an account presenting itself as the project.
- **Merchandise**: clothing, stickers, hardware, printed matter.
- **Hardware, appliances, images or services sold with the mark**, including a machine advertised as shipping with LiberNT, a hosted or cloud image, paid support, or a paid build.
- **Anything that states or implies endorsement, partnership, certification or official status**, including "official", "certified", "powered by LiberNT" on a product, and use of the mark in a company name.

Permission is often granted, and it is free. Ask.

## Rules that apply to every use

- Do not alter the logo: no stretching, cropping, recolouring, rotation, drop shadows, gradients, added text or outlines. Use the published asset at its own proportions, with clear space around it.
- Write the name as **LiberNT**: one word, capital L, capital N, capital T. Not Libernt, LiberNt, LIBERNT, Liber NT or Liber-NT.
- Use the name as an adjective over a noun where it reads naturally ("the LiberNT kernel"), and do not pluralise or conjugate it.
- Do not use the marks in a way that disparages the project, in unlawful material, or in material a reasonable reader would take as coming from us.
- Do not register the mark, a translation or a transliteration of it, or anything confusable with it, as a trademark, company name or domain anywhere in the world.

## Enforcement and permission

We enforce this policy, including through hosting providers, package registries, app stores and domain dispute procedures, and through copyright in the logo artwork.

To request permission, or to report a misuse, write to **arif.ing@outlook.com** with the use you have in mind, where it will appear and for how long. We answer in plain language and we say yes to most honest uses.

If anything here is unclear for the use you have in mind, ask before you ship. That costs you one email and saves everyone a dispute.
